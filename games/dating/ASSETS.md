## Animation sources

Two CC0 clip libraries from Quaternius, both on the same rig, kept as the no-root-motion GLB only (the FBX and `_RM` root-motion copies were left out of the vendor folder).

- `UAL1_Standard.glb` (43 clips). - `UAL2_Standard.glb` (43 clips). Useful: `Idle_FoldArms_Loop` (Vesper, Frost), `Idle_No_Loop`, `Yes`, `Idle_TalkingPhone_Loop` (Prince on a "call", the ex calling in), `Consume`, `Idle_Rail_Call`, `Idle_Rail_Loop`, `LayToIdle`, `Hit_Knockback`.

**Rig, for retargeting.** Quaternius "universal" humanoid with Unreal mannequin names, 65 joints:
`root > pelvis > spine_01 > spine_02 > spine_03 > neck_01 > Head`; arms `clavicle_l > upperarm_l > lowerarm_l > hand_l` with fingers `thumb|index|middle|ring|pinky_01.._03_l` (plus `_04_leaf_l` end bones); legs `thigh_l > calf_l > foot_l > ball_l (> ball_leaf_l)`; same with `_r`. Rest pose is a T-pose (hand_l at x 0.74, y 1.44), height 1.83 m, pelvis at y 0.917. It faces **+Z** with its left on +X. The `root` bone carries a -90 degree X rotation (a Blender Z-up armature) and `pelvis` has a further ~104 degree X rotation, so local bone axes do not match VRM's: retarget with rest-relative world rotations, never by copying local quaternions, and push the pelvis translation track through `root`'s rest matrix before using it.

Bone map to VRM humanoid names (VRM 0.x thumb naming):
`pelvis>hips, spine_01>spine, spine_02>chest, spine_03>upperChest, neck_01>neck, Head>head, clavicle_X>XShoulder, upperarm_X>XUpperArm, lowerarm_X>XLowerArm, hand_X>XHand, thigh_X>XUpperLeg, calf_X>XLowerLeg, foot_X>XFoot, ball_X>XToes, thumb_01/02/03_X>XThumbProximal/Intermediate/Distal, index|middle|ring|little_01/02/03_X>XIndex|Middle|Ring|LittleProximal/Intermediate/Distal` (`pinky` maps to `Little`; X is `left`/`right` with `_l`/`_r`).

Method: the three-vrm Mixamo example (`loadMixamoAnimation.js` in the pixiv/three-vrm repo) works unchanged in shape: for every rotation track, `q' = parentRestWorld * q * inverse(restWorld)` from the source rig's rest pose, written to the VRM's *normalized* bone; then for a VRM 0.x model negate the x and z of every quaternion and of the hips position; scale the hips position by `vrmHipsY / 0.917`. Drop tracks whose target bone is missing (several avatars have no finger or thumb bones).

**Retargeting risk: medium.** The cast is not human-proportioned: the pyramid and toaster have stick limbs on a box body, Tilly is a chibi with a head a third of his height, Vesper is a fish. Clips will mostly read, but expect arms through bodies and sliding feet on the odd shapes. Use clips for walking, sitting and idle-talk, and do the gestures that carry the comedy (lean in, shrug, wince, slump, tiny victory) procedurally on the normalized humanoid bones, which all 16 models share.

## Risks

1. **VRM 0.x, faces -Z.** All 16 are VRM 0.x (UniVRM 0.99). Load with `@pixiv/three-vrm` v3 (`GLTFLoader` + `VRMLoaderPlugin`), call `VRMUtils.rotateVRM0(vrm)` so they face +Z, and call `vrm.update(delta)` every frame (spring bones and expressions). Retargeted clips need the 0.x x/z sign flip described above.
2. **Expression names change at load.** The manifests list the names stored in the files (VRM 0.x presets). three-vrm v3 exposes them under VRM 1.0 names: `a>aa, i>ih, u>ou, e>ee, o>oh, joy>happy, sorrow>sad, fun>relaxed, angry>angry, blink>blink, blink_l>blinkLeft, blink_r>blinkRight`. There is no `surprised` preset on any model (Stonks Reporter has an unbound `Surprised` morph you can drive directly). The `lookup/down/left/right` presets are empty on all but Good Knight, and only the spare Ajo Majo has eye bones, so look-at must turn the head bone, not the eyes. Cool Pyramid has no `blink_r`.
3. **Mixed materials and the cut.** 13 of 16 use MToon (with a `KHR_materials_unlit` fallback); Astro Nacho (NAV), Math Book and Possum Enjoyer use plain lit PBR. Under the same studio lights NAV will shade differently from everyone else. Register the MToon plugin (default in three-vrm), then either convert those three to MToon or force one shading model across the cast. Check that "the light goes out" actually darkens MToon characters: MToon is toon-shaded with a flat shade colour and can stay readable with lights off, so the cut may need a material tint or emissive drop as well as a light change.
4. **Scale and anchors.** Raw heights run from 1.12 m (Leaf Boy) to 2.02 m (Stonks Reporter and Sport TV, both including hood or antennae); the manifests' `height` is the full bounding box, hats included. Place name tags, emote marks and look-at targets from the head bone's world position (head heights vary from 0.66 m to 1.74 m before scaling), not from the bounds. All are T-posed in rest; put them in an idle pose before the first frame is shown.
5. **Weight.** 16 models total 74 MB (78 MB with preview images); the 11 cast models 49 MB (1.2 to 6.7 MB each, PNG textures up to 3.7 MB). Load only the five in the episode and prefetch the next cast. Downscaling textures to 1K would roughly halve each file if needed.
6. **Licence record.** R3 is CC0 as stated in each file's VRM meta (`licenseName: CC0`, CC0 URL, `creditNotation: UNNECESSARY`, `modification: ALLOW`, commercial use allowed) and in the registry's `projects.json`. The meta `author` field is a deployer wallet address (`0xded150f6...`), not a name; the registry credits Polygonal Mind. Credit them anyway.
7. **Sound gaps.** No CC0 "aww" or "gasp" was found (OpenGameArt has none; freesound needs a login). The "ooh" file is a few voices and may sound spooky. Record the team for aww, gasp and ooh, or synthesise them. Music is CC-BY 4.0: the credit line at the top of this file must be shown. None of the music is trimmed; only `Funk Game Loop` is built to loop, the others need loop points or a fade.
